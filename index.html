Robot learning beyond Earth’s physics

Asif Amin
University of California, Riverside
Current project: Learning in Zero Gravity Google Scholar
Vision-language-action models (VLAs) learn from data collected under Earth’s physics, mostly on a tabletop. I study what happens when that physics changes, and how to use altered physics on purpose: as a source of more diverse training data, as a test of how well VLAs adapt, and as the setting for robots that will one day work in space.

Idea 1

Zero-gravity training for better performance on Earth in progress

Train robot experts with gravity switched off until they touch the object, then distill their spatially diverse demonstrations into VLAs to improve ordinary, Earth-gravity manipulation. Project page.

1
Zero-G RL experts
objects float until touched; gravity then switches on
2
Diverse demonstrations
reaching, grasping and pushing throughout 3D space
3
VLA distillation
fine-tune a VLA (π0.5) on the demonstrations
4
Earth-gravity tests
spatially harder versions of the same tasks

Compared against fixed and adaptive gravity randomization (OpenAI-style ADR) at the same cost.

Prior work

Domain randomization varies physics as a robustness perturbation, around Earth gravity (OpenAI et al., 2019).

What would be new

Gravity as a controllable distribution shift that is used as the main training regime, not as a perturbation. Preliminary results: at heights only the zero-gravity experts trained on, they reach the object 3.4× and grasp it 4.2× as often as the best gravity-trained or gravity-randomized expert.

Idea 2

Adapting Earth-trained VLAs to microgravity planned

The reverse direction: take a VLA trained on Earth-gravity data and make it work when the physics changes, as in orbit.

1
Earth-trained VLA
pretrained, terrestrial data
2
Microgravity tasks
same scenes, different dynamics
3
Adaptation
few-shot fine-tuning, dynamics-aware conditioning
4
Altered-gravity evaluation
measure what breaks and what transfers

Prior work

Space-domain work has so far adapted models to visual or task shift under Earth-like dynamics: OpenVLA fine-tuned for in-orbit inspection (VLMs as Operator Agents in the Space Domain, 2025); lunar autonomy with a fine-tuned vision-language agent and lighting adaptation (SELENE).

What would be new

A dynamics shift rather than a visual one, with VLA manipulation actually evaluated under altered gravity.

Idea 3

A corrector VLA: actor and critic co-trained proposed

A second VLA watches the first one act and corrects it, either in language (“move left”) or directly in embedding space. The two are trained together.

1
Actor VLA
proposes an action chunk
2
Corrector VLA
sees the observation and the proposed chunk
3
Correction
language (“lower, then left”) or an embedding-level adjustment
4
Corrected action
executed; outcomes train both models
↺ outcomes feed back into both models

Prior work

Adversarial imitation trains a discriminator against the policy (GAIL, DAC). Language corrections from humans steer policies at run time (“No, to the Right”; CorrectVLA), and an expert teacher can distill into a VLA on-policy (VLA-OPD).

What would be new

A co-trained peer that fixes failures from out-of-distribution physics, where no human or ground-truth teacher is available. Zero gravity is the driving case.

Idea 4

Metric language grounding inside containment volumes proposed

Train VLAs on commands with real units and 3D references, such as “move 2 inches left” or “go to the top-right front corner of the box”, to sharpen their spatial ability.

1
Containment volume
a known 3D frame around the workspace
2
Metric commands
distances, directions and volume-relative positions
3
Auto-labeled demonstrations
labels computed from end-effector motion
4
Metric-supervised VLA
tested on ordinary, non-metric spatial tasks

Prior work

Direction-only language motions (RT-H); metric spatial reasoning in vision-language models rather than action models (SpatialVLM, RoboTracer); language-to-slot placement through a rendered visual goal (AnySlot).

What would be new

End-to-end metric supervision of the action model, tested for transfer to non-metric tasks, plus references relative to a 3D volume. Without a table or a “down”, volume-relative metric commands are how you would instruct a robot in space.

Idea 5

A generalist VLA for space manipulation long-term vision

A pretrained, multi-task VLA for manipulation in space: a “π0.5 for space”.

1
Space-manipulation dataset
simulation plus analog hardware
2
Benchmark
tasks × gravity × lighting
3
Multi-task pretraining
one model across tasks and conditions
4
Space operations
servicing, assembly, sampling

Prior work

Existing space work fine-tunes for single tasks (VLMs as Operator Agents in the Space Domain, 2025; SELENE).

What would be new

To my knowledge no generalist exists yet. The bottleneck is data, so a space-manipulation dataset and benchmark is the most valuable first step.

Idea 6

Physics curricula early idea

Schedule the physics itself during training: start where exploration is easy (zero gravity, low friction) and move toward the target physics as the policy improves.

1
Zero gravity
easy exploration of 3D space
2
Reduced gravity
partial support
3
Earth gravity
the target task
4
Varied friction and mass
robustness at the end

Prior work

Automatic domain randomization widens physics ranges as performance improves (OpenAI et al., 2019).

What would be new

Ordering physics regimes by how much they help exploration, rather than widening one range around Earth values.

Idea 7

Energy-aware action trajectories early idea

Train VLAs to avoid redundant motion: score demonstrations and action chunks by energy and wasted movement, and prefer efficient ones.

1
Demonstrations
from experts or teleoperation
2
Efficiency scoring
joint work, jerk, path length, idle motion
3
Filtering and regularization
keep or up-weight efficient behavior
4
Efficient VLA
smoother, faster, lower-power execution

Prior work

Smoothness and energy terms are common in RL reward shaping and trajectory optimization, while VLA training data is usually left unfiltered for efficiency.

What would be new

Efficiency as a data-quality axis for VLA training, which matters most where power is scarce, as in space.

Idea 8

World-model “zero-g minigames” for VLA practice early idea

Learn a world model of zero-gravity dynamics, then let VLAs practice short, game-like tasks inside it.

1
World model
learned zero-gravity dynamics
2
Minigames
catch, stabilize, push to a target
3
Practice in imagination
cheap rollouts for the VLA
4
Transfer
to simulation, then hardware

Prior work

Agents trained inside learned world models (DreamerV3); learned interactive simulators (UniSim, Genie).

What would be new

Targeted, game-like curricula for an unfamiliar physics regime, where real data is hardest to get.

Idea 9

ImagineAct: visualize before acting early idea

A VLA that first imagines the outcome, as a predicted image or subgoal, and then acts toward it.

1
Instruction and observation
2
Imagine
predict a future frame or subgoal image
3
Act
action chunk conditioned on the imagined goal
4
Check
compare imagination with what happened

Prior work

Goal-image generation for control (SuSIE); video prediction as a policy (UniPi); video-pretrained manipulation policies (GR-1).

What would be new

Using the gap between imagination and outcome as a signal, for example to detect when the physics differs from what the model expects.

Idea 10

VLMs as judges of agent-generated environments early idea

Let language-model agents generate simulation environments and tasks, and use a vision-language model to judge which ones are valid, solvable and useful.

1
Agent-generated environments
scenes and tasks written as code
2
Rendered rollouts
what the environment actually does
3
VLM judge
physically valid? solvable? diverse?
4
Curated library
training environments for VLAs

Prior work

Generative simulation and task generation (RoboGen, GenSim); LLM-written reward code (Eureka).

What would be new

Visual verification of generated environments, catching the physics and geometry bugs that text-only checks miss.
